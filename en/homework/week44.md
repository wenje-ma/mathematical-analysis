> **Theorem 3.2 (Cauchy's Formula for Radius of Convergence)**<br>$\frac1R=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sqrt[n]{\left|a_n\right|}$.

> **Theorem 3.3 (d'Alembert's Formula for Radius of Convergence)**<br>If $\lim\left|\frac{a_n}{a_{n+1}}\right|=R$, then the radius is $R$.

> **Theorem 3.4 (Uniform Convergence on Closed Intervals)**<br>Power series converge uniformly on closed intervals inside the radius, and can be differentiated and integrated term by term.

## 11.3-1-1

Find the radius of convergence of $\sum\frac{3^n}{n!}x^n$.

### Solution 11.3-1-1

$R=+\infty$. $\blacksquare$

## 11.3-3-1

Find the sum of $\sum\left(n+1\right)x^n$.

### Solution 11.3-3-1

$\frac1{\left(1-x\right)^2}$. $\blacksquare$

---

## 11.3-3-3

Find the sum of $\sum n^2x^n$.

### Solution 11.3-3-3

$\frac{x\left(1+x\right)}{\left(1-x\right)^3}$. $\blacksquare$

## 11.3-5

Prove: for any $A> \frac1R$, there exists $C> 0$ such that $\left|a_n\right|\le CA^n$.

### Solution 11.3-5
